About

I climb trees and help manage Eugene's urban canopy for a living. I'm in pursuit of higher education at Oregon State University studying Natural Resources and Geography. My GIS work begins with questions from the field. Where is root disease spreading? Where is canopy cover missing in communities that need it most? I use GIS as a tool to answer these questions, but my work starts in the field, primarily within the urban canopy and wildland interface.

I'm currently in an accelerated Master of Natural Resources program at Oregon State finishing my B.S. and MNR in tandem. My capstone proposal uses lichen density as an air quality proxy. I'm hoping to flag priority sites for tree planting through an environmental justice lens.

The throughline to my pursuits is people. Trees and plants help make up the natural infrastructure of our lives and landscapes but are often an afterthought in community planning and participation. I want my work to help change that: surfacing where canopy is missing, where risk is concentrated, and where a planting decision now helps a neighborhood build future resilience. Good data should help people understand and shape the landscape they live in. Good science should be communicable to all and not gatekept by technical jargon.

Oak Root Disease & Mediterranean Oak Borer

Undergraduate research assistant on a Forest Engineering, Resources & Management study asking whether existing root disease in Oregon white oaks makes them more attractive to the invasive Mediterranean oak borer. Plot surveys, tree health assessments, sonic tomography, and wood sampling across Willamette Valley sites.
